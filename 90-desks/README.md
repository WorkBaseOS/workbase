# 90-desks — work in a field of its own

Most of your work is your role: the responsibilities you carry, in operations; the defined pieces of work you deliver, in projects; all of it read against the brain, which knows the business, the team and the people you answer to. A desk is for the part of your work that is a different world: an expertise you still practise yourself, with its own context, that does not fit the operations and projects of your role. The department head who still does the engineering. The CEO who still advises clients. The operator who also teaches, treats, designs or codes. That work lives here so everything stays in one Workbase, but it keeps its own context: its own charter, its own standards, its own way of working, read on its own terms rather than through the brain of your role. One desk per field.

**What belongs here:** work in a field of its own, done by you, that would be misread in the context of your role.
**What never belongs here:** work of your role that happens to need your skill. Writing the client proposal is an operation's work; being a writer is a desk.

A desk works in two ways:
- **As a workspace:** you do the work right here, in the desk folder.
- **As a control room:** the work lives somewhere else — a code repository, a design tool, a clinic's system — and the desk holds the context, briefs, links, and key outcomes.

**The one rule:** each desk has a charter (how this field is worked); the rest is your work, and your drafts are your business. A charter holds four things: the field, where the work happens, your standards, and what of the brain applies here, if anything. Ask your assistant to set up a desk and it creates one properly, charter first.

One place to manage everything, without having to do — or store — everything in one place.
