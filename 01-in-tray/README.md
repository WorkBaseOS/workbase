# 01-in-tray — the tray

Toss things here all day without breaking stride: meeting notes, a screenshot, a PDF, an exported report, half a thought in a text file, an email or Slack message that needs follow-up. Anything, any format, by any route — drag it in yourself, save it from an app, or have a connector you've set up deliver things here. Once something lands, it becomes part of your work like everything else.

**What belongs here:** whatever arrived and isn't handled yet.
**What stays here:** nothing — the tray is a buffer, not a home. Say **"process my tray"** and your assistant organizes what you've added, updates the state of your work, and asks when something needs clarification.
**The one rule:** you never have to decide where something goes — that's what processing is for. (Filing things directly yourself is fine too; the tray is just the no-thinking-required option.)

**From the chat:** drag a file into the conversation, or type a note, and say **"to my tray"** — your assistant saves it here, dated, for the next "process my tray". Say **"process this"** instead and it is handled on the spot. Dictation works the same way; audio files need transcribing first.

## How items are handled

Your assistant handles the tray **with you, one item at a time** — it says what an item is and what it would change, you decide; or you dictate ("record that …", "put an action on me to …", "that's done") and it writes exactly that. For a rich item, "let's go through it point by point". Nothing is recorded without your word, and "look at X with me" records nothing at all.

The conventions it follows:
- **Never guess.** A destination, a date, a commitment, or a completion it isn't sure of becomes a question. It never invents a deadline.
- **State goes in the overview, content goes in a working document.** A decision, a commitment, a change, a risk → the container's `_overview.md`. The content of the work — objectives, an outline, a plan → a document at the top of the container, named for what it is, started when content first arrives.
- **One home for state.** The container most responsible holds it; others get at most a one-line note. If an overview already reflects an item, it just files it.
- **Filing.** The item goes into the container's `received/` folder, name unchanged. A pure status message whose content is now recorded goes to `99-archive/` — unless an overview cites it. Nothing is edited or stamped.
- **No home yet?** It proposes one — an operation for ongoing work, a project for work with a finish line — and creates it on your yes.
- **Not sure what something means?** It stays in the tray marked `unclear--` with a question. One unclear item never blocks the rest.
- **Things that don't add up** — numbers that disagree, dates that conflict — are shown to you, never resolved silently.
- **Sensitive content** is filed with its work like anything else; it says so, and the constitution governs anything drafted from it.
- **Long transcripts** get a one-page meeting record first — decisions, commitments, open points — filed with the work.
