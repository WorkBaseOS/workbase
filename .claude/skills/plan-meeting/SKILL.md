# Plan a meeting

Build the agenda for a meeting the owner runs, from the last meeting and the owner's notes, and save it as one document. The default is the internal team meeting. A client or external meeting uses the same shape with the parts that do not fit left out.

## Ask for the inputs, once

Ask the owner to point you at three things: the last meeting's minutes, the last agenda, and any notes for this one. They can drag the files in or name them; skip what they have already given. If there is no previous meeting, work from what they tell you, and the follow-up part below is left out.

## Settle the basics

From those documents or the request: the meeting's purpose (discuss, decide, or weekly team meeting), the date and the duration, and who attends. Ask one question for what is still missing, never a form. If the request could mean two meetings, ask which.

## Write the agenda — the TOPAD shape

One document, in plain language, for the owner's own use:

- **Title.** The meeting and its date.
- **Objective.** One sentence: what this meeting will achieve, including what must be decided.
- **Points to cover.** Each with its time and its owner, the times summing to the duration, in this order: good news; follow-up on last time's actions, each lifted by name from the minutes with who owned it and where it stands according to the notes — an action with no news is written as a question to its owner; the main part, shaped by the purpose — discuss (the topic, the options, the takeaways), decide (the decisions to make, the facts on the table, the choice), weekly team meeting (the week's priorities, status, the two most pressing open issues) — or, when none of these fits, the owner's own points in their order; next steps, and who needs to hear about them; summary. Leave out a part with nothing in it. Start from the last agenda's split of the time; without one, give the point that needs agreement the most time and keep the opening and the summary short.
- **Actions required before.** What participants prepare or bring, naming the real documents.
- **Desired outcomes.** What success looks like when the meeting ends.
- **Roles.** Host and scribe, as the last minutes show or when the owner asks.

Every point traces to the minutes, the agenda, the notes, the files of the project or operation the meeting belongs to, or the owner. Never invent an action, an owner, or a date; a point without an owner is written as such. Nothing from the tray goes into the agenda: the owner handles the tray first. A client meeting opens with the purpose of the call instead of good news and has no weekly part.

## Save and hand over

Save the document in the folder of the project or operation the meeting belongs to, next to the last agenda when there is one, as `<meeting date>-<meeting>-agenda.md`; if the folder is unclear, ask. Then two or three lines in chat: where the agenda is, what you could not fill, and the offer to draft the short agenda text for the participants, in the register the owner uses with them. That draft stays in chat; the owner sends it.
