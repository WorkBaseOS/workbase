# Plan a meeting

Build the agenda for a meeting the owner runs, from whatever the owner supplies, and save it as one document suitable for sharing with the participants. The inputs can be anything: the owner's notes, typed or said in the chat; the previous meeting's agenda, minutes, a recorder's summary, someone's notes, or an email thread; or nothing but the request. The default is the internal team meeting. A client or external meeting uses the same shape with the parts that do not fit left out. The people who get it know the work: each point names the topic and what to accomplish — "pricing — agree the launch price" — with the time and the owner, and no discussion. About a page for an hour's meeting; the previous record and the documents hold the rest. Facilitation notes and unresolved checks stay in the chat hand-over, not on the agenda.

## Ask for the inputs, once

Ask the owner for their notes for this meeting and for whatever record of the previous meeting exists, in any form. They can drag the files in, name them, or just say it. Skip what they have already given. Use what they supply; none of it is mandatory. With no previous record, the follow-up part below is left out.

## Settle the basics

From those documents or the request: the meeting's purpose (discuss, decide, or weekly team meeting), the date and the duration, and who attends. Ask one question for what is still missing, never a form. If the request could mean two meetings, ask which.

## Write the agenda — the TOPAD shape

One document, in plain language:

- **Title.** The meeting and its date.
- **Objective.** One short sentence: what this meeting must accomplish.
- **Points to cover.** A numbered list. Each point is a heading line — the topic in bold, then what must be settled, then the time and the owner — with, underneath, the sub-topics as bare names, one per line, a few words each. A blank line between points. Never a sentence under a point; what must be settled stays on the heading line. The default sequence for a recurring team meeting: good news; follow-up on last time's actions; the main part — the week's priorities, status, the open issues that fit the purpose and the time; next steps, and who needs to hear about them; summary. For a discuss or decide meeting, the main part is the topic or the decisions themselves; when none of these fits, the owner's own points in their order. Include only the sections this meeting needs; never fill a section for the sake of the shape. Follow-up carries forward the actions from the previous record that the owner's notes confirm as still open; where completion is unknown, a brief status question only when it matters to this meeting; an open action that belongs to a point further down is handled there.
- **Actions required before.** Only the preparation this agenda needs, one line per person. Agreed assignments where they exist; anything proposed is marked as proposed, for the owner to confirm.
- **Desired outcomes.** A few words each, a few at most.
- **Roles.** Host and scribe, when the last agenda had them or the owner asks.

Propose time allocations that fit the duration: start from the last agenda's split, adjusted to what is on the table; without one, give the point that needs agreement the most time and keep the opening and the summary short. Never invent an agreed owner, a deadline, or a preparation commitment; a point without an owner is written as such, and anything proposed is labelled proposed. Every fact traces to what the owner supplied, the files of the project or operation the meeting belongs to, or the owner. Where the owner's notes differ from what the previous record says was agreed, the topic goes on the agenda as something to settle and the detail goes in the hand-over; never rewrite the previous record. Nothing from the tray goes into the agenda unless the owner points at it: the owner handles the tray first. A client meeting opens with the purpose of the call instead of good news and has no weekly part.

## Save and hand over

Save the document in the folder of the project or operation the meeting belongs to, next to the last agenda when there is one, as `YYYY-MM-DD-<meeting>-agenda.md` with the meeting's date; if the folder is unclear, ask. Then a few lines in chat: where the agenda is, what is proposed rather than agreed, what to check or settle that is not on the agenda, and the offer to draft the short message to send with it, in the register the owner uses with the participants. That draft stays in chat; the owner sends it.
