# Plan a meeting

Build the agenda for a meeting the owner runs, from the last meeting and the owner's notes, and save it as one document. The default is the internal team meeting. A client or external meeting uses the same shape with the parts that do not fit left out. The people who get it know the work: the agenda names the topics, the owners, and the times — never what a thing is or why. Under 250 words for an hour's meeting; the minutes and the documents hold the rest.

## Ask for the inputs, once

Ask the owner to point you at three things: the last meeting's minutes, the last agenda, and any notes for this one. They can drag the files in or name them; skip what they have already given. If there is no previous meeting, work from what they tell you, and the follow-up part below is left out.

## Settle the basics

From those documents or the request: the meeting's purpose (discuss, decide, or weekly team meeting), the date and the duration, and who attends. Ask one question for what is still missing, never a form. If the request could mean two meetings, ask which.

## Write the agenda — the TOPAD shape

One document, in plain language, for the owner's own use:

- **Title.** The meeting and its date.
- **Objective.** One short sentence: what this meeting must settle.
- **Points to cover.** One line each: the topic — the time — the owner. The times sum to the duration. In this order: good news; follow-up on last time's actions — only the open ones, each a few words with its owner and date, an action with no news as a question to its owner, an open action that belongs to a point further down handled there; the main part, shaped by the purpose — discuss, decide, or weekly team meeting (the week's priorities, status, the two most pressing open issues) — or, when none of these fits, the owner's own points in their order; next steps, and who needs to hear about them; summary. Under a point that covers several topics, sub-points as bare topic names, a few words each — never a sentence. Leave out a part with nothing in it. Start from the last agenda's split of the time, adjusted to what is on the table; without one, give the point that needs agreement the most time and keep the opening and the summary short.
- **Actions required before.** One line per person: what they bring.
- **Desired outcomes.** A few words each, a few at most.
- **Roles.** Host and scribe, when the last agenda had them or the owner asks.

Every point traces to the minutes, the agenda, the notes, the files of the project or operation the meeting belongs to, or the owner. Never invent an action, an owner, or a date; a point without an owner is written as such. Where the notes differ from what the minutes record as agreed, make it a point in a few words; never rewrite the minutes. Nothing from the tray goes into the agenda unless the owner points at it: the owner handles the tray first. A client meeting opens with the purpose of the call instead of good news and has no weekly part.

## Save and hand over

Save the document in the folder of the project or operation the meeting belongs to, next to the last agenda when there is one, as `YYYY-MM-DD-<meeting>-agenda.md` with the meeting's date; if the folder is unclear, ask. Then two or three lines in chat: where the agenda is, what you could not fill, and the offer to draft the short agenda text for the participants, in the register the owner uses with them. That draft stays in chat; the owner sends it.
