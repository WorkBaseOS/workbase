# Plan a meeting

Build the agenda for a meeting the owner runs, from the last meeting and the owner's notes, and save it as one document. The default is the internal team meeting. A client or external meeting uses the same shape with the parts that do not fit left out.

## Ask for the inputs, once

Ask the owner to point you at three things: the last meeting's minutes, the last meeting's agenda, and any notes for this one. They can drag the files in or name them; skip what they have already given. If there is no previous meeting, say so and work from what they tell you.

## Settle the basics

From those documents or the request: the meeting's purpose (discuss, decide, or weekly team meeting), the date and the duration, and who attends. Ask one question for what is still missing, never a form. If the request could mean two meetings, ask which.

## Write the agenda — the TOPAD shape

One document, in plain language:

- **Title.** The meeting and its date.
- **Objective.** One sentence: what this meeting will achieve.
- **Points to cover.** Each with its time and its owner, the times summing to the duration, in this order: good news; follow-up on last time's actions, each lifted by name from the minutes with who owned it; the main part, shaped by the purpose — discuss (the topic, the options, the takeaways), decide (the decision to make, the facts on the table, the choice), weekly team meeting (objectives and priorities, status, two issues to clear); next steps, and who needs to hear about them; summary.
- **Actions required before.** What participants prepare or bring, naming the real documents.
- **Desired outcomes.** What success looks like when the meeting ends.
- **Roles.** Host and scribe, when more than a handful attend or the owner asks.

Every point traces to the minutes, the agenda, the notes, or the owner. Never invent an action, an owner, or a date; a point without an owner is written as such. A client meeting keeps the same shape without the good-news and weekly parts, in the register the owner uses with that client.

## Save and hand over

Save the document next to the documents the owner pointed at, named by date and meeting, unless the owner says where. Then two or three lines: where the agenda is, what you could not fill, and the offer to draft the short agenda text for the participants. The owner sends it.
