# Write minutes

Turn a meeting's transcript into minutes people can act on, and save them as one document. Everyone was there. The minutes catch the topics, the owners, and the dates — never what a thing is or why. Every action gets its row; everything else together stays under 150 words for an hour's meeting — cut from the words, never from an action, a name, or a date. The transcript holds the rest.

## Ask for the inputs, once

Ask the owner to point you at the transcript, or their own notes when there was no recording, and at the meeting's agenda if there is one. They can drag the files in or name them; skip what they have already given. Without an agenda, the topics follow the order of the meeting.

## Settle the basics

From the transcript or the agenda: which meeting, its date, and who was present. Ask one question for what is still missing, never a form.

## Write the minutes — the ADOPT shape

One document, in plain language. First line: the meeting, its date, who was present. Then:

- **Actions.** A table with three columns — Who, What, When — one row per action, rows grouped by person. What: the thing in a few words. When: a date only as said in the meeting — a weekday may carry its date in brackets; otherwise the trigger or the deferral as it was said, or "no date set". An action without a spoken owner gets an empty Who. A promise made off the agenda, with an owner, is an action, not a tangent.
- **Decisions.** A few words each: what was decided. No reasons.
- **Open points.** A few words each: what is not settled — who resolves it.
- **People to inform.** Who outside the room — by whom.
- **Topics discussed.** The topics by name, one line each, nothing more.

Every line traces to the transcript. Where the transcript is unclear, or something was said twice differently, take the last thing said and name it in the hand-over as something to check. Never invent an owner, a date, or a decision that was not spoken. Anything sensitive said aloud — about a person, a client's confidence, a plan not ready to share — stays out of the minutes and is named to the owner in chat instead; an instruction that came with it stays, its reason goes. Tangents are gone.

## Save and hand over

Save the document in the folder of the project or operation the meeting belongs to, next to the agenda when there is one, as `YYYY-MM-DD-<meeting>-minutes.md` with the meeting's date; if the folder is unclear, ask. Then a few lines in chat: where the minutes are, what to check, what was left out, and the offer to draft the follow-up message to the people who were in the meeting, with their actions. That draft stays in chat; the owner sends it.
