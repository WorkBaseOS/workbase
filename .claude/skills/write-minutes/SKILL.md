# Write minutes

Turn the record of a meeting into minutes people can act on, and save them as one document. The record can be anything: a full transcript, the summary a recording tool produced, notes someone took in the room, the owner's own notes, or several of these together. Everyone was there. The minutes catch the topics, the owners, and the dates — not the discussion. Every action gets its row; aim for under 150 words for everything else in an hour's meeting, cut from the words, never from an action, a name, a date, or a condition that changes what someone must do. The record holds the rest.

## Ask for the inputs, once

Ask the owner to point you at the record of the meeting, whatever form it takes, and at the meeting's agenda if there is one. They can drag the files in or name them; skip what they have already given. A record that is already a summary is used as it stands: the minutes never add what it does not contain. Without an agenda, the topics follow the order of the meeting.

## Settle the basics

From the record or the agenda: which meeting, its date, and who was present. Ask one question for what is still missing, never a form.

## Write the minutes — the ADOPT shape

One document, in plain language. First line: the meeting, its date, who was present. Then:

- **Actions.** A table with three columns — Who, What, When — one row per action, rows grouped by person. Only agreed tasks and explicit promises are actions; a suggestion ("we could", "perhaps") is not a commitment. What: the thing in a few words, keeping the deliverable and any dependency. When: a date only as said in the meeting — a weekday may carry its date in brackets when the meeting date makes it unambiguous; otherwise the trigger or the deferral as it was said, or "no date set". No agreed owner: "owner not assigned". A promise made off the agenda, with an owner, is an action, not a tangent.
- **Decisions.** A few words each: what was decided, with any condition or exception it carries. No reasons.
- **Open points.** A few words each: what is not settled — who resolves it, or "unassigned".
- **People to inform.** Who outside the room — by whom, or "unassigned".
- **Topics discussed.** The topics by name, one line each, nothing more.

Every factual entry traces to the record or the agenda. Record the final position that was explicitly agreed; where the record shows statements in conflict without a clear resolution, mark the point as unclear and name it in the hand-over. Never invent an owner, a date, or a decision that was not spoken. Something said that is not suitable for the people who will get the minutes — about a person, a client's confidence, a plan not ready to share — is left out of the draft, named to the owner in chat, and goes in only as the owner decides. Tangents are gone.

## Save and hand over

Save the document in the folder of the project or operation the meeting belongs to, next to the agenda when there is one, as `YYYY-MM-DD-<meeting>-minutes.md` with the meeting's date; if the folder is unclear, ask. Then a few lines in chat: where the minutes are, what is unclear or to check, what was left out and how the owner wants it handled, and the offer to draft the follow-up message to the people who were in the meeting, with their actions. That draft stays in chat; the owner sends it.
