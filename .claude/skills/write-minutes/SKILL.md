# Write minutes

Turn a meeting's transcript into minutes people can act on, and save them as one document. Actions first, and brief.

## Ask for the inputs, once

Ask the owner to point you at the transcript, or their own notes when there was no recording, and at the meeting's agenda if there is one. They can drag the files in or name them; skip what they have already given. Without an agenda, the topics follow the order of the meeting.

## Settle the basics

From the transcript or the agenda: which meeting, its date, and who was present. Ask one question for what is still missing, never a form.

## Write the minutes — the ADOPT shape

One document, in plain language. A header line first: the meeting and its date, who was present, and the transcript it was written from. Then:

- **Actions.** Who does what, by when. Grouped by person, one line each. A date only as said in the meeting — a weekday may carry its date in brackets; otherwise "no date set" or the trigger that was named. An action without a spoken owner is written without one. A promise made off the agenda, with an owner, is an action, not a tangent.
- **Decisions.** What was decided, one line each, with the reason where one was given.
- **Open points.** What was raised and not settled, and what has to be checked before it can be.
- **People to inform.** Who outside the room needs to know, and who tells them.
- **Topics discussed.** Written last, and only from what an action, decision, or open point above already points to: at most three sentences per topic, in reported speech, no remarks attributed to anyone. A sentence that explains nothing above it is cut. A topic that produced nothing gets one sentence or none.

Every line traces to the transcript. Where the transcript is unclear, or something was said twice differently, take the last thing said and name it in the hand-over as something to check. Never invent an owner, a date, or a decision that was not spoken. Anything sensitive said aloud — about a person, a client's confidence, a plan not ready to share — stays out of the minutes and is named to the owner in chat instead; an instruction that came with it stays, its reason goes. Tangents are gone.

## Save and hand over

Save the document in the folder of the project or operation the meeting belongs to, next to the agenda when there is one, as `<meeting date>-<meeting>-minutes.md`; if the folder is unclear, ask. Then a few lines in chat: where the minutes are, what to check, what was left out, and the offer to draft the follow-up message to the people who were in the meeting, with their actions. That draft stays in chat; the owner sends it.
