# Delegation scan

Most of what stays on an owner's plate stays out of habit. The scan puts every task through the same four questions and gives each one a destination. The owner decides; the table is a conversation starter, not a verdict.

## The plate

Gather what the workspace shows with the owner's name on it — actions in meeting minutes, priorities in `00-brain/this-quarter.md`, commitments in project plans and operation notes, open items in `HANDOFF.md` — and show the list in one block: "this is what the files put on your plate; add what they never see, strike what is gone." The recurring things live in the owner's head, not the files: the weekly agenda, the approvals, the escalations, the invoicing. Ask for them once. With a thin workspace, ask for the list outright. Nothing is scanned until the owner has seen the list.

## The team

Read `00-brain/team.md` for who is there and what they own. For the scan, each person also needs what they want to grow into and how much room they have right now. Ask once, briefly, only for what the brain does not hold; write the answers into `team.md` on the owner's word, so the next scan does not ask again. Never invent a strength, a wish, or a capacity. An owner with no team has the other destinations; say so and go on.

## The four questions, every task

1. **Expertise** — does this genuinely need the owner's own skill or standing?
2. **Growth** — would doing it develop someone, or build something repeatable?
3. **Time and value** — is this the best use of the owner's time?
4. **Fit** — is there a home for it: a person with the room and the interest, the assistant, someone outside, or nobody?

## The destinations

- **You** — only what needs the owner: the client relationship, the judgement call, the work that is the business.
- **A person** — a named team member; partial when the owner keeps a part, said which.
- **The assistant** — the Workbase takes it: name the skill that already does it, or the house rule, template, or connector that would, or a new skill to build with the native builder.
- **Outside** — a freelancer, a bookkeeper, a vendor.
- **Nobody** — it stops.

## The table — in chat

One row per task: the task · needs you? · grows anyone? · best use of your time? · fit · recommendation with the destination named, and for partial what the owner keeps. Then three lines under it: what the owner gets back if they take the recommendations, what stays with them and why, and what has no home right now. Honest about "no fit"; on a small team that will be common, and the assistant is often the fit.

Every judgement rests on what the files and the owner said; where the scan cannot tell, it asks in the row rather than guesses.

## The handover — on request, one task

"Pick one." The how follows the destination:

- **To a person:** the handover conversation in seven steps, in the owner's register, as lines the owner can say — open it and say why them · the task and what done looks like · the resources and who to go to · when you will check in, without checking up · say early when something is off · this is yours · close with confidence. Questions where the owner has not said something, never invented.
- **To the assistant:** the setup, concretely — which skill, what to say to it, or which house rule or template to add, or what a new skill would need to know; built when the owner says.
- **Outside:** a short brief — the task, done means, by when, what they need from the owner.
- **Nobody:** one line for the quarter document's stop list, offered.

Nothing is recorded in a project's or operation's documents until the owner says where; then the handover goes there in one line — who took what, from when.

## Close

A few lines: the table stands, the one handover prepared if asked, what was written to `team.md`, and anything the owner could not answer, left as theirs.
