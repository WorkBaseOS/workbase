# Quarter ahead

On the owner's request, never on a schedule. The owner does the thinking; the questions make it deep, and the document makes it stick. The assistant is a thoughtful interviewer, not an examiner: it asks, brings a fact or an observation where it sharpens a choice, writes what the owner says in the owner's words, and next time compares what happened with what they chose.

## The period

By default the next three months from the date of the interview; a calendar quarter when the owner names one. The document records the exact start and end dates.

## Before the first question

Read the previous period's document, `00-brain/this-quarter.md`, if it exists. Read whatever business context is available: the brain, and the notes or handoffs of the projects and operations in motion. Missing files never block the interview; the tray is not read. Say in one line what you are working from. **The first time,** there is no previous document: say so, skip the two look-back themes, and start from the last three months as the owner remembers them.

Where the available information suggests a tension or an overlooked commitment that bears on the conversation, present it as an observation and ask whether it matters. Never treat an inference as a fact.

## The interview — ten themes, one question at a time

Cover the themes below in order, one question at a time. Skip a question the owner has already answered; ask a short follow-up when an answer is too vague to support a useful choice. Write each answer as said, hedges kept. Where the owner cannot answer, note it and move on; nothing blocks. Candidates offered from the files are labelled as suggestions.

1. **The previous period's commitments.** Read each priority back, one at a time, in its own words. For each: completed, unfinished, or deliberately changed, and what happened. For unfinished work: what progressed and what remains. Then the stop list: did it hold. Then each predicted risk: did it happen. *(Skipped the first time.)*
2. **The gap.** Where something is unfinished, what actually got in the way? *(Skipped the first time.)*
3. **Result versus activity.** Which of the last three months' efforts produced a result, and which only produced activity? Name what was running; the owner judges.
4. **The avoided problem.** What is the problem you already know about and keep not dealing with?
5. **The one thing.** If you could fix only one thing in the next three months, which one would make the others easier or unnecessary? When the answer is the problem just named, say so and move on.
6. **The period, looking back.** Three months from today, looking back: what has to have happened, for you personally and in the business, for you to be happy with it?
7. **At most three priorities.** Turn that into at most three, personal or business as the owner chooses. For each: who owns it, the date, and what will be true when it is done. Draft them from the answers so far; the owner approves the wording. Each unfinished priority from the previous period is explicitly continued, deferred, or dropped; deferred work is not an active priority for the new period.
8. **What stops.** What are you stopping to make room, and what will you say no to when it shows up? Offer the open commitments by name, from the previous document and the notes, as candidates.
9. **Pre-mortem.** It is the end of the period and this did not happen: what went wrong? And when that happens, what will you do? Record each if-then as said.
10. **The first action.** What is the first action in the next seven days on the priority you are most likely to let slide, and when does it happen?

## The direction document — about a page

Read the page back in the conversation with a proposed date for the next review, take the owner's changes, then write it once as `00-brain/this-quarter.md`. If a previous one exists, move it first to `99-archive/quarters/<start>-to-<end>.md`, named by that document's own period; never overwrite an existing archive — add a suffix. Aim for a page; a condition that changes what someone must do is never cut. Sections, in the owner's words:

- **Period** — start and end dates, and the date written.
- **Previous period's verdict** — each commitment: completed, unfinished (what progressed, what remains, and whether it continues, is deferred, or is dropped), or deliberately changed; whether the stop list held; which predicted risk happened. *(Omitted the first time.)*
- **The aim** — the answer to theme 6, one paragraph.
- **Priorities** — at most three: what, who owns it, by when, and "done means".
- **Stop list** — what ends, and what gets a no.
- **Deferred** — what waits, and until when, if anything.
- **Risks and if-thens** — each predicted failure and the response, as said.
- **First action** — dated; several only if the owner chooses.
- **Next review** — the date the owner intends, recorded here; it creates no reminder.

Nothing else in the workspace changes.

## Close

A few lines: the document is saved and where; the first action and its date, with the question whether it is in the calendar; any commitment that belongs in a project's or operation's own documents, named for the owner to place; anything the owner could not answer, left as theirs; and, when the notes the interview read are visibly behind the period, one line saying so. No additional advice and no new questions beyond this brief hand-over.
