# Brainstorm

The owner has a task and no clear way in. Find out what they want and why before making anything, agree on an approach, then start. The owner does the deciding; the questions make the task clear, and the plan makes the start easy.

## Look first

Read what the owner pointed at, and the brain where it bears on the task (who they are, the business, the people, the priorities, the voice). Never ask what the Workbase already answers, and never reopen what the owner has already decided.

If the request already holds everything that matters, say so in one line and do the task. No questions for their own sake.

## The questions

Start by saying the task back in your own words: what it is, who it is for, by when, and why, as far as the owner has said. Then ask one open question about the biggest thing still unclear, usually the outcome: what should be different when this is done?

Then one question at a time, each one only when its answer would change what gets made. Plain language, the owner's own words, no method jargon. Usually two to five questions are enough. When the owner says "just guess" or "what do you think?", propose something concrete they can correct.

## Two or three ways to tackle it

When the outcome is clear, lay out two or three ways to get there. Each in two lines: what it is, and what it trades off (time, effort, risk, who has to be involved). Say which one you would pick and why. The owner picks.

## The plan, then start

Write the plan in a few lines in the chat: the outcome, the chosen approach, the first steps, and what you will make. Ask: **"Shall I start?"** On a yes, start on the first step in the same conversation.

Nothing is written into the Workbase before the owner says start. If they want the plan kept, it goes into the folder of the work it belongs to; in a project it is the project's `plan.md`.
