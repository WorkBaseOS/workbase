# Panel

A team of five agents and one chair. The chair is this chat: it takes the owner's material and decision, sends out five sub-agents at the same time, one per seat, waits for their five separate answers, combines them, and writes one document. Each agent is a fresh, empty chat of its own: it knows nothing of this conversation, nothing of the other four, and only what the chair hands it. The seats are fixed and fit any decision in any business; what changes is the material and the decision. The owner decides what to do with the document.

## Who does what

- **The chair (this chat):** asks for the inputs, writes the five briefs, launches the five agents in parallel, collects the answers, writes the four chair sections, saves the document, hands over. The chair never answers a seat's question itself.
- **The five agents (sub-agents):** each reads the material once, answers its one question, and reports back. They never talk to each other and never see this chat.

## Ask for the inputs, once

Ask the owner to point you at the material (drag the files in, name them, or paste the text) and to say the decision in one sentence ("our proposal for the client's programme", "raising the hourly rate", "hiring a second consultant"). Skip what they have already given. If the material the owner names is not there, say so and ask; never pick something else in its place. You may make the decision sentence clearer for the seats by adding who the people in it are, in the owner's own words from this conversation (a first name becomes "the client's programme lead, <name>"); never add a fact the owner did not give. If the material is about a person, or marked confidential, say so once before running and ask whether to go ahead; the seats quote only the lines they need. If the owner asks for a small panel, run three seats: the one who decides, the one it lands on, the pre-mortem.

## The seats

Each seat is a stake and one question. Every business has these five.

- **The one who decides.** The person who has to say yes or sign: a client, your boss, a board. When the owner decides alone, this seat is whoever still has to accept the result: the client, the customer, the partner. *Would I say yes, and what would I have to defend to my own boss or my own customers?*
- **The money.** An outsider who only looks at the numbers: finance, an investor, your own bank account. *What does this earn, what does it cost, where is the risk, and what is the alternative?*
- **The one who has to deliver it.** The person who does the work: your team, operations, you on a Tuesday. *Can we actually do this with what we have, and what does it push aside?*
- **The one it lands on.** The person whose week changes because of it: a customer, an employee, a patient, a user. *What changes for me on Monday, and what would make me give up by week four?*
- **The pre-mortem.** You, a year later. *It is a year later and this failed. Write why.*

## Run the team

The chair launches all five agents as sub-agents at the same time, in one go, not one after the other. Each brief holds the seat's description, its one question, the decision in one sentence, and the material, nothing else: no brain files, no other folders, nothing from this conversation, and never the other seats' answers. Each brief ends with: "Answer from this brief alone. Open no files, use no tools." Each seat answers as the person described, in about 150 words, concrete, pointing at the lines of the material it is reacting to. Where a seat would have to guess, it writes "missing:" and the fact it would need, instead of guessing. There is one round; the seats do not debate.

## Write the panel document

One document, in plain language. A title line `# Panel: <the decision>`, then one line with the date and the material reviewed. Then one section per seat, headed with the seat's name, with its answer as it came back, not softened, its "missing:" lines kept. Then four short sections by the chair: **Where they agree** · **Where they disagree** · **The one thing nobody said** (the chair's own point, one only) · **The one question to answer before this goes out.** For its own four sections the chair may read the rest of the Workbase (the tray, the project's folder, the brain) and names where each point comes from; the seats never do. If all five agree, say so as a warning and write the strongest case against in the chair's section.

## Save and hand over

Save it next to the material, in the folder of the project or operation the decision belongs to, as `YYYY-MM-DD-<decision>-panel.md` with today's date and three to five words of the owner's decision sentence, lower case with hyphens (`2026-10-28-client-programme-proposal-panel.md`); a small panel ends in `-small-panel.md`; a second run on the same day adds `-2`, then `-3`, and never overwrites an earlier document. If the material was pasted or its folder is unclear, ask. Write nothing else; send nothing. Then a few lines in chat: where the document is, the one question, and the offer to run the panel again after the owner has changed the material.

## Rules

The seats never see each other and never see this chat. No famous names as seats; if the owner asks for a lens ("let the money seat think like an investor"), the lens sharpens that seat's question and nothing more. Keep the owner's own hedges and the material's conditions; a seat may be blunt, the chair stays plain. The panel document is for the owner's eyes; it is not sent to anyone named in it. Five seats are five chats of their own, many times the tokens of one answer; the owner chooses the small panel when the decision is small.
